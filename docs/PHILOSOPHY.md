# Glyt Philosophy

## Why Glyt exists

Today's programming experience often makes people learn the machine's preferred way of thinking before they can express their own ideas.

Glyt reverses the priority.

The human starts with intent. The language should meet the human there, then reveal deeper machinery as needed.

## Easy does not mean shallow

Glyt should reduce memorization and pointless ceremony without reducing depth.

The preferred educational unit is a meaningful, understandable concept that can be experienced, manipulated, and connected to other concepts.

A user should increasingly be able to generate their own answers because they understand what is happening, rather than because they memorized the wording of a rule.

## Time is not the enemy

A project taking time is not automatically a problem.

Time spent exploring, learning, designing, testing, or making a large project is meaningful.

The enemy is dead friction: time consumed by barriers that contribute nothing to understanding or creation.

## There are no “beginner features” that must stay beginner-only

A newcomer may want to do something ambitious immediately.

A veteran may want to play around like a newcomer again.

Glyt therefore should not treat beginner and expert as separate species. It should provide multiple paths to the same underlying capability.

## Human creativity stays in the loop

Glyt may help interpret intent, explain mistakes, suggest alternatives, or make safe defaults.

But the person's intention remains central.

The system should amplify agency, not replace it.

## Alive software

Glyt should make software creation feel more connected to the user's curiosity, goals, experimentation, and sense of possibility.

“Alive” does not mean noisy, childish, or attention-hacking.

It means the process feels like something a human can inhabit rather than a ritual they must endure.

## The anti-brainrot rule

Glyt should optimize for users leaving more capable, not for users staying longer.

Closing the editor after a successful project is not a failure state.
