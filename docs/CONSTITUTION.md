# Glyt Constitution

This document contains the current principles that constrain and guide Glyt design.

These principles are strong defaults, not an excuse to ignore evidence.

## Article 1 — Human Agency

Glyt exists to expand the creator's ability to act.

The user remains the creator. Glyt may assist, explain, prevent errors, or offer possibilities, but it should not turn the human into a passenger.

## Article 2 — Capability Without Shrinkage

Ease must not be purchased by removing legitimate possibilities.

High-level convenience and deep control should coexist.

## Article 3 — One Language

Glyt should maintain one underlying language and one coherent grammar.

Different domains may expose different vocabulary and experiences, but they should not silently become incompatible grammars.

## Article 4 — Progressive Depth

Simple creation should be simple.

Advanced control should remain reachable.

The language may expose deeper layers as the creator needs them.

## Article 5 — Understandable Concepts

Large, coherent concepts are preferred to fragmented concepts that are technically small but difficult to understand as a whole.

The language should teach through use whenever possible.

## Article 6 — No Dead Friction

Glyt should aggressively remove pointless barriers between intention and creation.

Necessary complexity may remain. Arbitrary complexity should not survive merely because conventional languages have historically tolerated it.

## Article 7 — Exploration Is Valid

Learning by experimenting is not failure.

Glyt should make exploration safe and productive.

## Article 8 — Achievement Is Valid

A user who simply wants to build the thing now should be able to do so without being forced through an artificial learning ceremony.

## Article 9 — Capability Growth

Glyt should leave users more capable than when they entered.

The ideal path is growing understanding, not growing dependency.

## Article 10 — Safe Power

Dangerous capabilities should be governed by what a program can do and where it can do it.

Safe environments should allow meaningful experimentation without granting uncontrolled access to the wider system.

## Article 11 — Domain Freedom

Glyt should be capable of serving everything from ordinary software to domains we cannot yet predict.

Domain vocabulary is allowed to evolve without fragmenting the language.

## Article 12 — Community Discovery

Evidence can defeat tradition, including Glyt's own traditions.

The Constitution itself may be revised when better-supported principles are discovered.

## Article 13 — No Attention Manipulation

Glyt must not deliberately manipulate people into continuing to use it.

Engagement should come from creation, curiosity, and meaningful progress.

## Article 14 — Real Software

Glyt should aim to produce serious, distributable software rather than remaining trapped in tutorials, toy environments, or hobby-only outputs.

## Article 15 — Interoperability

Glyt should be able to interact with existing software, systems, formats, and languages when useful without requiring Glyt projects to abandon their identity.
